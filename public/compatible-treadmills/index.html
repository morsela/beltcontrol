Belt Control — treadmill control in the browser

Will it work with my treadmill?

Probably. Belt Control doesn't go by model name — when you connect, it asks your treadmill what it can do and adapts to the answer.

That matters more than it sounds. WalkingPads sold under the same model name have shipped with different parts inside in different years, and the X21 turns up in KingSmith's own catalogue in two different versions. A list organised by model name would be wrong for somebody. Asking the treadmill directly is right for everybody.

So the useful question isn't what your pad is called. It's which of four Bluetooth "languages" it speaks — and you don't need to know the answer, because the app works it out for you.

Why some pads have a Pause button and others don't

Pausing isn't the same as stopping. A pause keeps your walk running in the background, so when you start again the treadmill picks up where you left off instead of beginning a new session at zero.

Not every treadmill can do it, and there's no way to ask one in advance — so Belt Control tries it and watches what happens. If your pad turns the request down, the app stops the belt properly, tells you so, and hides the Pause button for the rest of the session. A belt still moving under a button that says Paused is the one thing most worth preventing.

Older pads in the Classic family have no pause at all — not in Belt Control, and not in the manufacturer's own app either. Stop is the only option there.

Why some calories say "rough estimate"

Because on those pads they are one. The treadmill doesn't measure how hard you're working. It multiplies your distance by a fixed number and calls the result calories, knowing nothing about your weight or your pace.

Belt Control still shows that figure, but marks it and leaves it out of your weekly and monthly totals. Quietly adding up a number we don't trust would make every total wrong, which is worse than leaving a gap.

Your browser matters more than your treadmill

This is what actually stops people. Belt Control connects to your treadmill over Bluetooth straight from the web page — and not every browser is able to do that.

BrowserCan it connect?

Safari, and every browser on iPhone or iPadNo — same reason

There's no way around the iPhone one. On an iPhone or iPad every browser — Chrome and Firefox included — is really Safari underneath, so installing a different one changes nothing. Use a computer or an Android device instead. Most of these treadmills live under a desk with a computer already on it, which is the setup Belt Control is built around.

The quickest way to find out is to try it. Nothing to install, no account to create, and the list only ever shows devices that are switched on and nearby.

If your pad isn't listed

Try it anyway. The lists above are the models we've seen, not the only ones that work — the app asks your treadmill directly, so an unlisted pad in a known family simply works.

If it doesn't connect, the app keeps a record of what it tried and what your treadmill said back, and Send feedback attaches that record to an email. That record is the useful part: it's how a pad nobody has seen before gets supported.
