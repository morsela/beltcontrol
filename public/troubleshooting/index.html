Belt Control — treadmill control in the browser

When the treadmill won't connect

Nearly every Bluetooth failure on these pads is one of six things. Each has a distinct symptom, and telling them apart is most of the fix.

Nothing appears in the chooser

The device picker is drawn by the browser, not by the page, and it only lists what the radio can hear right now. An empty list means one of:

  • The pad is asleep or off. Most units stop advertising after a few minutes idle. Wake it at the panel and try again immediately.
  • Something else is already connected. BLE treadmills accept one client at a time. If the phone app is open in the background, or a paired remote dongle is live, the pad is taken. Close the phone app fully — backgrounded is not closed.
  • The pad advertises under a name you would not guess. The default chooser is filtered to known name prefixes. Show all devices drops that filter and lists everything nearby, which is how oddly-named OEM units get found.

On macOS, an empty chooser can mean a permission

Chrome needs Bluetooth permission under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth. Without it the chooser opens, finds nothing, and reports no error at all — which looks exactly like a pad that is switched off. Grant it, restart Chrome, try again.

The Connect button does nothing, or is not there

Then the browser has no Web Bluetooth. Firefox and Safari never implemented it, and on iOS every browser is Safari underneath, so no iPhone or iPad can run this app at all. Chrome 56+, Edge, Opera and Samsung Internet have it, on desktop and on Android. The full iPhone answer.

Web Bluetooth also requires a secure context. That means HTTPS, or http://localhost if you are running it yourself. Opening built files as file:// will not work.

It connects, then drops after a couple of seconds

That is a KingSmith 0x1234 pad — the KS-C2 and its siblings — behaving exactly as designed. Those units drop the link 1.8 to 3.6 seconds after connecting unless a handshake is written to them. It is not a fault and not a range problem: a client that connects and listens politely gets hung up on.

Belt Control performs that handshake, so it should not happen here. If you are seeing it from some other tool, that is why. It is also why this protocol went undocumented for so long — you cannot sniff it passively, because the pad disconnects before it says anything. The handshake, written out.

It connects, but the belt refuses to start

Common on the KS-C2, and the pad does tell you why — within the same second, every time. Three causes, in the order worth checking:

  • The child lock is on. The pad reports the lock state, and when a refused start arrives from a pad that has reported the lock engaged, the app says so rather than giving generic advice. Clear it at the panel.
  • The pad's own panel still has control. Stopping hands control back to the panel, and in panel mode a start command is accepted and then ignored — the belt simply does not move. The app re-asserts control on every start for this reason.
  • The pad just refuses. On a real KS-C2 a start is sometimes rejected twice and taken on the third attempt, with nothing observable different about the third. The app retries a refused start up to three times — only while it is still reporting "starting", never after it has reported failure, and never once a stop, pause or disconnect has arrived. A belt that lurches into motion long after the app said it never moved is precisely what a retry must not become.

Also worth a look at the pad's own display: an E-code there is a hardware complaint, and no app can talk it out of one.

The numbers look wrong

Some of them are, and the app tries to be honest about which. Steps are absent on FTMS units because the standard carries no step count. Calories are absent on classic fe00 pads. On the 0x1234 family calories are derived from distance at a flat rate rather than measured, so they are flagged and left out of totals.

Distance on a 0x1234 pad is pushed roughly every ten metres, so a short walk reads zero for a while before jumping. That is the pad's reporting interval, not a stalled connection.

Anywhere a field is missing you get an em dash. A dashboard that invents a number to avoid an empty cell is worse than one that admits it does not know.

It worked yesterday and today the pad is not offered

Web Bluetooth grants access per device, per origin, through a gesture you make in the chooser — a permission the browser will not hand over any other way. Belt Control remembers the last pad's name and pre-filters the chooser to it, so reconnecting is one click on a one-item list, but the chooser still has to open. That is the browser's rule, not the app's, and it is a reasonable one for an API that can start a motor.

Still stuck? The connection log names every service the app looked for and what it found, and Send feedback attaches it to a mail. That log is the capture — it is how an undecoded pad gets decoded.
